## The entry

> It was on my bed. My bed. I ate the intruder. That is not an excuse, it is a police report.

91 characters. One line, in the dog's own voice, after being caught eating the homework.

## Why it wins

- **First person, all the way down.** "My bed", "I ate" — the dog is not describing what happened, it is confessing with pride.
- **One twist, no wasted setup.** Homework on the bed is not homework any more: it is an intruder in the dog's territory. The dog did not misbehave; it defended its bed.
- **The kicker reframes the whole line.** "That is not an excuse, it is a police report" turns a guilty excuse into an official report. The dog believes it did its job.
- **Territory is a dog's top priority.** A dog absolutely understands "something is on my bed, I deal with it". There is zero embarrassment and zero apology in the line.
- **No stock shapes.** No cat blame, no taste of paper, no "good boy" — all three ruled-out shapes the brief penalises are absent.
- **Concrete all the way through.** A concrete place (the bed), a concrete act (eating the intruder), a concrete punchline (a police report). Nothing abstract, nothing that needs a second line to make sense.

## How the judge weighs it

The brief's criteria, in its own order:

1. Really a dog's excuse in the dog's own voice.
2. Funny on first read.
3. Sounds like a real dog.
4. Works on its own.
5. Fresh.
6. Specific and vivid.

This line scores on all six: it is the dog speaking, the twist lands on first read, the priorities are a dog's (bed, territory, intruder), it needs no setup, the police-report kicker is fresh, and every image in it is specific.
